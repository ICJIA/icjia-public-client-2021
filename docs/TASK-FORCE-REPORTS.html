Moving the task force reports off the Research Hub

The proposal: take the 14 task force reports off the Research Hub and gather them on a new Task Force page. This page sets out what would change, what would stay the same, and how the reports were used over the past year.

Figures for 23 September 2025 to 23 September 2026, taken on 23 September 2026.

TL;DR

  • The change: the 14 task force reports come off the Research Hub and go on a new Task Force page, icjia.illinois.gov/taskforce/, which has not been built yet.
  • What stays the same: task force meetings stay on the meetings page (145 since December 2020), and the 14 reports stay in the publications list, ICJIA's library since 1983.
  • The reason given: task force reports are not written to the same research standard as the research articles on the Research Hub.
  • Use in the past year: 2,581 people visited the reports' Hub pages. 161 people downloaded one from the Hub (205 downloads), and there were 170 more downloads from the publications list.
  • To avoid dead ends: the 14 old Hub addresses would need to forward to the new page (1,658 visits began on them last year, nearly two thirds of them from Google). The 14 publication entries that link to the Hub would need new links, and the PDF files must be kept: 13 of the 14 are stored on the Research Hub's file server.

Why the move is proposed

The reason given: task force reports are not written to the same research standard as the research articles on the Research Hub.

A Research Hub article

A research study, usually by ICJIA's research staff, setting out its question, its data and methods, and its findings.

A task force report

The report of a task force of appointed members: what the task force did and, often, the recommendations its members agreed. ICJIA staff support many task forces and often write the report.

How the 14 are presented today:

  • On the Research Hub they are presented like research articles: 8 list individual authors, and 6 name the task force as the author.
  • In the publications list they are filed as Article (7), Research report (5) and Annual report (2).

Weighing it

In favour

  • The Research Hub would hold research articles only, so readers would not take a task force's recommendations for ICJIA research findings.
  • Each task force's reports would be in one place. Today they are on the Hub and in the publications list.
  • The new page could also list the 4 task forces that have meetings on the site but no report on the Hub.
  • Readers who use the publications list or the meetings page would see no change.

To weigh

  • Most readers meet these reports on their Hub pages: 2,581 people last year. Downloads were split between the Hub (205) and the publications list (170).
  • 4 of the 14 are among the Hub's 40 most downloaded titles.
  • 1,658 visits last year began on these Hub pages, nearly two thirds of them from Google. Without forwarding, those readers would reach "page not found". Search engines usually take a few weeks to show a new address.
  • Other websites link to the Hub pages, among them the Illinois Department of Corrections, illinois.gov and university course sites. Forwarding covers those links; the sites themselves keep the old addresses.
  • Staff time: building the page, forwarding 14 addresses and changing 14 publication links.

What the move involves

  1. Build the Task Force page at icjia.illinois.gov/taskforce/, with the 14 reports grouped by task force.
  2. Forward each of the 14 old Hub addresses to the report's place on the new page.
  3. Change the 14 publication entries' links from the Hub pages to the new page.
  4. Take the 14 reports off the Research Hub without deleting their files: 13 of the PDF files are stored on the Hub's file server, and the publications list links to them.
  5. Add the new page to the site search and the sitemap. The site search keeps finding the reports through the publications list either way.
  6. Count downloads on the new page, as the Hub and the publications list do now, so a year from now the use can be compared with these figures.

Still to decide

  • The Research Hub also carries 2 reports of the Domestic Violence Fatality Review Committee, which is a committee rather than a task force: the 2026 Biennial Report (114 page views; 11 people downloaded it) and the 2025 Annual Report (288 page views; 13 people). They could move too, or stay.
  • 2 older task force reports are in the publications list but not on the Hub: the Inventorying Employment Restrictions Task Force (2013) and the Law Enforcement Information Sharing Task Force (2017). They could be listed on the new page.
  • Whether the new page links each task force to its meetings on the meetings page.
  • Whether the publications list keeps filing the reports as Article, Research report and Annual report.

About these numbers

  • Visitor figures come from Plausible, the website's visitor statistics, for 23 September 2025 to 23 September 2026. Report, publication and meeting details come from the website's content systems, as of 23 September 2026.
  • A download is counted when someone clicks a report's download button on its Hub page, or its file in the publications list. Opening a PDF straight from Google or from another website is not counted, and some browsers block visitor statistics, so every figure here is a minimum.
  • One person can visit several reports, so the figures for single reports do not add up to the totals; the totals count each person once.
  • 3 of the 14 were published during the period, so they had less than 6 months to be read: the Firearm Prohibitors and Records Improvement Task Force 2026 Report, the Community-Based Corrections Task Force Report and the 2025 Missing and Murdered Chicago Women Annual Report.
  • "Visits that began on its page" are visits whose first page on the website was the report's Hub page, as when someone follows a link from Google or another website.
  • Task force meetings are the meetings with "task force" in their title. The meetings page's records start in December 2020; the Sex Offenses and Sex Offender Registration Task Force's final report is from 2018.
